Time projection chamber: a track and its dE/dx

Ionization electrons drift to the end plates. Pads give x, y; the arrival time gives z; the curvature gives p; the collected charge gives dE/dx.

1 · Drift: time gives z

The particle crosses the chamber in nanoseconds. Its ionization electrons then drift parallel to E and B to the end plates at constant speed:

z = ±(L − vd(t − t0))

With L = 250 cm and vd ≈ 2.6 cm/µs the longest drift takes about 100 µs. Electrons from near the central electrode arrive last. Diffusion widens each cloud to a few mm (drawn ×12).

2 · Curvature gives p

Seen along the axis (End view) the track is a circle. Its radius from the pad hits gives

pT [GeV/c] = 0.3 · B [T] · R [m]

and the slope in the Side view turns pT into p. Here B = 0.5 T.

3 · Charge gives dE/dx

Each of the 80 pad rows measures the charge left in 2 cm of gas. Rare hard collisions make a long Landau tail, so the highest 40% of the rows are dropped and the rest averaged (truncated mean).

βγ = p / Mc  ⇒  dE/dx = f(p/M)

At the same momentum different masses sit at different βγ on the Bethe–Bloch curve, so the bands separate at low p.

What the model assumes

  • ALICE-like geometry, simplified: gas from r = 85 to 245 cm in 80 pad rows (ALICE: 159 rows of three sizes), half-length 250 cm, vd = 2.58 cm/µs, diffusion 220 µm/√cm.
  • 13.5 primary clusters per cm at minimum ionization, scaled with the mean loss; cluster sizes ∝ 1/k2.2 up to 2000 electrons.
  • Mean loss: ALEPH-type parametrisation of Bethe–Bloch, 50 at minimum ionization (the usual ALICE TPC units).
  • Not included: energy loss and scattering along the track, loopers after half a turn, gain variations, space charge, pile-up of tracks, particles stopping in the gas.

Geometry and gas: ALICE Collaboration, “The ALICE TPC”, NIM A 622 (2010) 316, arXiv:1001.1950.
