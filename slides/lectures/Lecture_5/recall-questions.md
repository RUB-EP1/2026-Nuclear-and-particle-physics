# Lecture 5 · Recall questions

Six multiple-choice questions on Lecture 4 (phase space, widths, cross sections and luminosity), for the board recap or a Menti quiz. They used to be slides 18–23 of Lecture 5.

## Question 1 — Amplitude, phase space or flux?

In $d\sigma=\overline{|\mathcal M|^2}d\Phi/F$, which statement is correct?

**A.** $d\Phi$ describes interaction strength; $\mathcal M$ counts states.

**B.** $\mathcal M$ describes the interaction; $d\Phi$ counts final states; $F$ normalizes incident flux.

**C.** Doubling beam intensity doubles the cross section.

**D.** Branching fraction is another name for incident flux.

> **Answer:** B. Intensity changes the rate, not the cross section for independent collisions at fixed energy.

## Question 2 — What happens at a two-body threshold?

Let $M\to m_1+m_2$ from above, with a finite, constant matrix element and nonzero daughter masses. What happens to the width?

**A.** It stays constant because the interaction is unchanged.

**B.** It diverges because the particles move slowly.

**C.** It tends to zero because $q\to0$ and $\Phi_2\propto q/M$.

**D.** It vanishes only if a conservation law forbids the decay.

> **Answer:** C. Γ=q|M|²/(8πM²) for an angle-independent amplitude and distinguishable daughters.

## Question 3 — Which width determines the lifetime?

A particle has $\tau=6.58\times10^{-22}\,\mathrm s$ and a channel with $B=0.20$.
Use $\hbar=6.58\times10^{-22}\,\mathrm{MeV\,s}$. What are $(\Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}},\Gamma_i)$?

**A.** $(1,\ 0.2)\,\mathrm{MeV}$

**B.** $(0.2,\ 1)\,\mathrm{MeV}$

**C.** $(1,\ 5)\,\mathrm{MeV}$

**D.** $(5,\ 1)\,\mathrm{MeV}$

> **Answer:** A. Total width ℏ/τ=1 MeV; partial width BΓtot=0.2 MeV.

## Question 4 — Luminosity and event counts

A process has $\sigma=2\,\mathrm{pb}$ and the sample has $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{int}}=10\,\mathrm{pb^{-1}}$.
If instantaneous luminosity doubles, which statement is correct?

**A.** The cross section becomes $4\,\mathrm{pb}$.

**B.** The same integrated luminosity now gives 40 produced events.

**C.** The event rate stays fixed, but the sample grows faster.

**D.** The sample gives 20 produced events; twice the luminosity collects it in half the time.

> **Answer:** D. Assume steady running at fixed energy, no branching or efficiency cuts.

## Question 5 — How many coordinates survive?

For a three-body decay with fixed masses, which statement is correct?

**A.** Nine independent coordinates remain after conservation laws.

**B.** Five remain: two describe shape and three orientation. Pair masses are unchanged by a Lorentz boost.

**C.** Three pair masses are independent, and change when the event is rotated.

**D.** A boost to the laboratory creates a sixth independent decay coordinate.

> **Answer:** B. 9−4=5 at fixed parent four-momentum. Three pair invariants obey one sum rule. Changing the parent lab motion is not an extra internal decay coordinate.

## Question 6 — Does a peak prove a resonance?

A constant matrix element generates a uniform Dalitz plot in $(s_{12},s_{13})$. Its projection onto $s_{12}$ has a broad maximum. What can explain it?

**A.** A resonance is required.

**B.** Lorentz invariance has been violated.

**C.** The allowed interval in $s_{13}$ varies with $s_{12}$.

**D.** A uniform two-dimensional density must have a uniform projection.

> **Answer:** C. Pure phase space can produce a broad maximum. Compare with the kinematic projection before interpreting structure as dynamics.
