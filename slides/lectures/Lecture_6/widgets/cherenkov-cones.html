Why both pictures are right

The backward cone is where the photons emitted at different times are now. The forward cone is which way each of them is going. Old photons are far out on the cone, young ones near its apex.

  1. A wavelet emitted at time te from xe has radius uτ at τ = t − te, with u = c/n. The particle is then vτ ahead of xe.
  2. The envelope runs from the particle, tangent to every circle. Emission point, tangent point and particle form a right triangle with hypotenuse vτ and leg uτ. The angle at the particle is μ, the angle at the emission point is θC: sin μ = uτ/vτ = 1/nβ = cos θC  ⇒  μ + θC = 90°
  3. The radius to the tangent point is normal to the front, and it is the path of the photon emitted at te: rγ = xex̂ + uτ (cos θC, ±sin θC),   vγ,x = u cos θC = u²/v > 0 So the photon always moves forward.
  4. Subtract the particle position xe + vτ, using u = v cos θC: rγ − rp = vτ sin θC (−sin θC, ±cos θC) = vτ sin θC (−cos μ, ±sin μ) The unit vector is the backward direction of the front. The photon sits on the front, a distance vτ sin θC behind the apex, and trails the particle by Δx = vτ sin²θC.
  5. Differentiate: the photon's velocity relative to the particle is v sin θC along the front, pointing backward. Turn on the velocity triangle: photon velocity = particle velocity + slide along the front. The front itself translates rigidly at v; its normal speed is v sin μ = u.

The “Follow particle” view only moves the camera with the particle. It is a Galilean shift of the lab picture, not the particle rest frame, and the relative velocity above is a lab-frame closing rate. The medium is taken as non-dispersive, so phase and group velocity are both c/n. Below threshold, nβ ≤ 1, the wavelets nest inside each other and no envelope forms.
